## 1. Figure typography — one knob for the whole project

**Why:** presentation review of the Summer 2026 deck; the verdict across every figure was "too
small". Asked for +2–4 pt everywhere.

**New file `figstyle.py`.** Named type-size roles (`TITLE`, `LABEL`, `TICK`, `LEGEND`, `ANNOT`,
`TABLE`, …) all derived from one `BUMP = 3` constant, plus the `PLOT_RC` rc-context. Every figure
writer imports it, so the next size change is one line instead of ~130 literals. The
depth-calibration figures had been sitting 1–2 pt below everything else and now share the scale, so
they grow by 4–5 pt rather than 3.

**Touched:** `run_sample.py`, `report.py` (`PLOT_RC` is now `figstyle.PLOT_RC`), `row_report.py`,
`calibrate_depth.py`, `extract.py`, `synth.py`, `porosity_table.py`.

**Revert:** `git checkout -- <files> && rm figstyle.py`. Behaviour is unaffected — no measurement
path touches typography.

## 3. Depth-calibration model selection — SCIENCE CHANGE, read before reverting

**Why:** review pointed out that the interaction model was being accepted on `adj_R² ≥ 0.10`, which
scores the whole fit. A band can clear that on the passes and speed main effects while its
`passes×speed` coefficient is indistinguishable from zero — and the figure then draws a curved
target contour whose curvature is entirely that unsupported term.

**Changes in `calibrate_depth.py`:**

- New `fit_additive_ols()` — `depth ~ 1 + passes + speed`, no interaction. Straight target contour.
- `choose_recommended()` now requires **p < alpha on the interaction coefficient itself** before
  keeping the interaction model; otherwise it drops to additive. Dose forms remain the fallback
  below both. Signature gained `addit` and `alpha`.
- `_predict()` takes the band-result dict instead of loose fits; new `_passes_for_target()`.
- `fig_heatmap()`: nothing is drawn outside the convex hull of measured settings — no field, no
  contour (fading it was not enough; a faded surface still reads as data). A band that swept only
  **two** speeds gets no field at all: two levels fix the ends of the speed response and say nothing
  about the shape between them, so it is drawn as measured cells plus the model's required-pass
  estimate at each measured speed.
- Report prints the interaction p-value, states that the contour's curvature rests entirely on that
  term, and flags any band with ≤ 2 speeds.

**Effect on the current data:** D50 (p = 0.64) and D100 (p = 0.41) switch from interaction to
additive — their 55 µm contours are now straight. D300 (p = 0.014) keeps the interaction but, having
only two speeds, shows point estimates (17 passes @ 400 mm/s, 38 @ 800) rather than a curve.

**Revert:** `git checkout -- calibrate_depth.py` — but note this reverts item 2's title change too,
and restores contours that assert curvature the data do not support.

## 4. Registration — `_adaptive_pin_mask` scope + statistic fix

**The bug (confirmed, not suspected).** `bb4d252` scaled the pin-prominence bar by
`DOSE_ROBUST_PROM_FRAC × span`, where `span` was the 95−5 percentile of relief over the **whole
scan**. On a single-cell scan — every wafer-row capture, which is what it was tuned on — that is the
cell's own relief. On a **multi-cell** sample the deepest cell sets the bar and shallower cells'
pins fall under it. A/B on `071626 D100 D50 4x4` (assemble once, register twice, only the constants
differing): **9/16 cells** with the shipped constants, **16/16** with the pre-`bb4d252` values. The
cut was a clean monotone threshold on depth — every cell ≤ 34.6 µm dropped, every cell ≥ 41.1 µm
kept — and the losses were exactly the low-dose cells the depth calibration needs.

Isolating the two constants: the **prominence** fraction causes it (9/16); the area floor is
innocent (16/16 with it alone).

**The change.** New `_local_pin_prominence()` measures, per **block** (~3 pin pitches, inside a unit
cell), the 95th percentile of relief — how high a pin top stands above its local background — and
the bar is `max(2 µm, DOSE_ROBUST_PROM_FRAC × that)`. Two things moved from the shipped version:

- **Scope**: block, not whole scan. This is the actual bug fix. A single-cell scan is unaffected by
  construction, which is why the wafer row is untouched.
- **Statistic**: 95th-percentile prominence, not the 95−5 peak-to-trough span. `rel` is height minus
  a local mean, so pins sit above it and the floor below; peak-to-trough is the whole excursion, not
  the pin, and it also collects any other relief in the block — on the 4×4 the un-etched array
  dividers put the span at ~24 µm where the pins stand ~12 µm. `DOSE_ROBUST_PROM_FRAC` now reads
  directly as "a pin counts once it is this fraction of the way up a real pin", and is
  **re-calibrated 0.20 → 0.25** for the new statistic (row 24/24 at 0.25, 22/24 at 0.20).

Docstring corrected: the helper claimed to feed "the fallback only", but `register_sample` calls it
on the **marker** path — that wrong comment is much of why the side effect was not anticipated.

**Verified:** wafer row 24/24 ✓ · 2×2 4/4 ✓ · selftest 280/280 ✓ · 4×4 **9/16 → 14/16**.

**New regression test** in `selftest.py` section [14]: a tiled synthetic scan with one 120 µm cell
spliced beside two 10 µm cells; all three must register. Confirmed it bites — 3/3 with the fix, 1/3
with the scan-wide span monkeypatched back in. Every other synthetic scan in the file is
uniform-depth, which is why this class of bug was invisible.

**Dead ends, recorded so they are not retried:**

- `max − min` over a pin pitch instead of percentiles. Not robust: on the debris-heavy
  `72026 D100 D50 2x2` it read spikes as relief and took that sample from 4/4 to **1/4**.
- Lowering the fraction. The 4×4 gives the same 14/16 at 0.12, 0.15 and 0.20, so bar height is not
  what blocks the last two cells.
- Lowering `PIN_AREA_FLOOR_FRAC`. Also 14/16 at 0.04, and it costs the row (22/24).
- An ejecta "knee" (apply the scaled term only above some relief). 22/24 at every fraction from
  0.25 to 0.50 with a 20 µm knee.

**Known limit — the last two cells.** Cells (2,1) and (4,1) of the 4×4, the two shallowest, stay
declined. This is not a tuning gap; the two populations overlap in relief:

| needs a 4–8 µm bar | prominence | must keep the 2 µm floor | prominence |
|---|---|---|---|
| row c1 D50 P100 P25_S400 | 16.5 µm | 4×4 (2,1) P10_S200 | 12.4 µm |
| row c6 D50 P100 P50_S800 | 22.4 µm | 4×4 (4,1) P10_S800 | 10.6 µm |
| row c4 D100 P150 P44_S800 | 32.3 µm | 4×4 (1,4) P80_S100 | 61.8 µm |

Between 12.4 µm and 16.5 µm the required bar rises 1.64× while prominence rises 1.33×, so no
proportional law separates them, and a law steep enough (quadratic) puts a 57 µm bar on the 61.8 µm
cell and kills that one instead. What distinguishes c1 from the 4×4's low-dose cells is **debris
texture, not depth** — c1 is debris-heavy at 16.5 µm of relief while the 4×4 cells are clean at
12.4. Closing it needs a debris-aware statistic, which is real work, not a threshold tweak. The
constant block in `register.py` carries this table so the next person does not re-derive it.
